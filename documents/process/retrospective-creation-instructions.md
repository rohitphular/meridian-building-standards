# Retrospective Writing Instructions

Write your retrospective after the epic is complete. An epic is complete when every story in the epic has its implementation approved — zero new findings and zero carry-forward in its final implementation review round. Focus on process, not technical detail.

---

## What to Cover

**What Went Well** — specific practices that worked. "Communication was good" is not useful. "Desk reviewed samples after each story and caught schema gaps before implementation" is.

**What Did Not Go Well** — problems and friction. No blame — focus on the process. Include both what happened and why it slipped through. Name the round and the specific failure.

**What Should We Continue Doing** — forward-looking commitments to keep practices that worked. Overlaps with "went well" but is a statement of intent, not just a past observation.

**What Should We Start Doing** — concrete changes for the next epic. Each item must be actionable: who does what differently.

**Action Items** — specific, owned, time-bound follow-ups. Not aspirations. An action item with no owner and no due date will not happen.

---

## Rules

- **Be specific.** Vague observations produce vague improvements. Name the round, the finding, the specific failure.
- **Focus on process, not domain.** "The formula was wrong" is not a process finding. "The formula was not dual-verified before submission" is.
- **Own your part.** Write about your own failures, not the other party's.
- **No minimum length.** A short, specific retrospective is better than a long, vague one.
